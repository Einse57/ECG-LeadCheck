## Deviations from the plan

- Amendment 1 was added after the first run and an independent methodological review, before this run (best-epoch selection, seeded augmentation, a second operating point, the null arm, Wilson intervals, patient bootstrap). The first run is unchanged in `study/archive_v1/`; in it one standard model reached 0.8998 clean macro-AUROC, just under the 0.90 bar, and its checkpoints were taken after the fold-9 peak.
- Bootstrap intervals are computed for the headline quantities only (LA/RA AUROC drop, LA/RA false-MI difference, the arm comparison), not for every cell; every cell has a Wilson interval in `RESULTS_per_model.md`.

## What this does and does not show

- It shows that the *simulated* consequences of electrode interchanges, applied to real PTB-XL recordings, move the outputs of standard deep-learning diagnosis models strongly, and how much at a fixed operating point.
- For the exact interchanges, undoing a correctly identified swap returns the original signal, so the recovery under detect-and-correct equals LeadCheck's detection and typing accuracy; the independent numbers are the cost on correctly recorded ECGs and the wrong-type corrections.
- The detector, the swap-augmented models and the test interchanges all come from the same simulator; right-leg interchanges use an approximation of the unrecorded right-leg potential.
- One dataset (PTB-XL) and two architectures. Real interchanged recordings from other hospitals have not been tested.
